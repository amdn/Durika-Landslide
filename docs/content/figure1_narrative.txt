# Figure 1 Sidebar: Technical Specifications

**Project:** Analysis: Cerro Dúrika Mass Wasting Event  
**Location:** Talamanca Range, PILA, Costa Rica  
**Coordinates:** Lat 9.3762° Lon -83.3009°  

**Personnel:**
* **Analyst:** Arturo Martín-de-Nicolás (Alumni BSEE/BACS Rice University)
* **Coordination:** Geógrafo José Rivas (Espacio Geográfico, Facebook)
* **Research Associate:** Gemini (AI Model) - Data Synthesis & Structural Modeling

### CHRONOLOGY
* **Dec 2015:** Baseline imagery (Fig 2) shows incipient ~200m "V" surface scoring; primary slope remains intact.
* **Apr 2025:** Satellite/Aerial imagery (Fig 1) captures the 220m vertical collapse; event occurs prior to this timestamp.
* **Dec 2025:** Ground-level summiting (J. Salas) via southern/eastern approach; failure remains hidden by northern topography.
* **Mar 2026:** Discovery via remote sensing (A. Martín-de-Nicolás); subsequent drone validation (J. Salas) confirms "ground truth."

### DATA PROVENANCE
* **Topography:** 1:50,000 IDECORI Isohypses (SNIT Geoportal)
* **Imagery:** Google Maps/SNIT (April 2025) verified against multi-temporal Google Earth Pro archives (Fig 2)
* **Orientation:** 180° Rotation (Gravity-Aligned / Slope-Down View)
* **Validation:** 2026 Drone Reconnaissance (Johel Salas)

### TOPOGRAPHIC BASELINE
* **Source:** 1:50,000 IDECORI Isohypses (SNIT Geoportal)
* **Interval:** 10 meters (Major index at 100m)
* **Status:** Pre-Event Photogrammetric Surface
* **Visualization:** Contours rendered in RED over the failure zone to illustrate the divergence between the historical stable slope and current satellite-observed morphology.

### OBSERVATIONS
* **Headscarp:** Massive structural failure initiating at the ~3,120m contour.
* **Displacement:** Approximately 220m vertical wedge collapse (from 3,120m to 2,900m base) channelizing into the headwaters of the Río Dúrika.
* **Secondary Event:** Reactive bank failure observed downstream, likely triggered by lateral erosion from primary debris pulse (Fig 3).

### FUTURE RESEARCH
* Volumetric quantification via Structure from Motion (SfM) utilizing recent 4K drone assets.
